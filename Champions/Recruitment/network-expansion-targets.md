# Data Day 2027: Network Expansion Targets

> **Audience:** internal / staff. Strategic recruiting list for reaching data people who are
> **not** already connected to Data Day, as a complement to
> [direct-ask-target-list.md](direct-ask-target-list.md), which works the existing network.
> **Status:** built 2026-09-14 from attendee history in
> `Archive/Data_Day_Historical_Reference.xlsx` plus public web research.
> **Contacts here are official and organizational only.** No personal addresses, and no
> people-search or data-broker sources.

---

## The two gaps this addresses

**Geographic.** MORPC serves 15 counties: Franklin, Delaware, Fairfield, Fayette, Hocking,
Knox, Licking, Logan, Madison, Marion, Morrow, Perry, Pickaway, Ross and Union. Across the
2025 and 2026 attendee lists (404 registrations, 143 organizations), **11 of those 15 counties
sent nobody.** Only Franklin is well represented, with Delaware, Licking and Knox appearing
thinly. Data Day is a regional event drawing from one county.

**Sector.** MORPC itself accounts for 70 of 404 attendee slots, roughly 17%. Outside that, the
attendee base is concentrated in city and county government, housing, and consulting. K-12 is
two organizations. Healthcare is essentially one. Utilities, state data operations, and data
infrastructure are absent.

## What we are targeting for, and what we are not

Content priorities for 2027 are **data governance, data management, data analysis, and the
infrastructure side** of data work.

**GIS is not privileged in 2027, and GIS people are still welcome.** The 2026 CFP gave GIS
first preference; 2027 does not. Ohio already has a large, established Ohio GIS Conference, run
by the County Engineers Association of Ohio and held in Columbus each September, and Data Day is
not competing with it.

The 2027 CFP is expected to drop the specific "we want GIS sessions" ask and instead offer a
**longer list of applicable tags and topics**, with GIS as one option among many. That is
inclusion by breadth rather than by preference, and it is also the answer to a real piece of
feedback: the 2025 post-event survey listed "more GIS" among suggested future topics. It did not
recur in 2026.

Practical consequence for this list: we target **data governance leads, data management and
reporting staff, analysts, and infrastructure operators**. Nobody is excluded for working in
GIS, but the county auditor GIS shop is not where we go looking first.

## Category 1: State data governance

**Best-aligned target on this list.**

Ohio DAS runs an **Office of Data and Efficiency** led by the **State Chief Data Officer**, who
chairs the **State CDO Council**. That council sets enterprise direction on data governance,
data management, and data analytics across state agencies. The InnovateOhio Platform holds
1,000+ datasets from 30 agencies under governed sharing agreements, alongside the public
DataOhio Portal.

They are headquartered downtown, minutes from MORPC, and have never appeared in a Data Day
attendee list.

| Target | Contact |
|---|---|
| Ohio DAS Office of Data and Efficiency | 30 E Broad St, 40th Floor, Columbus 43215 · 614-466-6511 · das.ohio.gov (the office page carries a contact form) |

**Ask fit:** a State CDO is credible as a **morning keynote** as well as a Champion. Worth
routing through the keynote pipeline first, since that ask closes October 31.

## Category 2: Data infrastructure operators

The **Ohio Technology Consortium (OH-TECH)** is the umbrella for the **Ohio Supercomputer
Center**, **OARnet** (the state's high-speed research network), and **OhioLINK**. These
organizations operate Ohio's public data infrastructure and sit on the OSU campus, close to the
venue. None appear in the attendee history.

| Target | Contact |
|---|---|
| Ohio Supercomputer Center | osc.edu |
| OH-TECH / OARnet / OhioLINK | oh-tech.org |

**Ask fit:** strong for the "what's next" lunch keynote thread, and a natural voice on the AI
and compute question the theme raises.

## Category 3: Education data management at scale

Not educators. The data operations layer beneath them.

**META Solutions** is a Columbus-based Information Technology Center serving **182 member
districts**, whose core service is EMIS reporting: Ohio's statewide education data collection,
accountability reporting, and funding distribution. Full-time data management, at regional
scale, entirely absent from Data Day.

Above it, the **Management Council of the Ohio Computer Network** coordinates all 16 ITCs
serving 1,000+ districts, and Ohio's **Education Data Professionals Network** connects
credentialed Regional Data Leads working inside ESCs.

| Target | Contact |
|---|---|
| Ohio Education Data Professionals Network (ODEW) | datanetwork@education.ohio.gov |
| META Solutions | metasolutions.net |
| Management Council of the Ohio Computer Network | managementcouncil.org |

**Ask fit:** CFP promotion. One email to the ODEW network can reach dozens of qualified data
staff, which makes this the **cheapest high-reach action on this list**.

**Warm path available:** the Educational Service Center of Central Ohio is already a Champion
organization through Audrey Altieri. Use her rather than cold outreach where the two overlap.

## Category 4: Local government data governance and IT

The same reframe as above: the technology and governance side, not mapping. The **City of
Columbus Department of Technology** already attends, 8 registrations across two years, and is
the proof case for this category. Extend to county IT directors, data governance leads, and
open-data portal owners across the region, prioritizing the 11 counties with no presence.

**Ask fit:** CFP promotion and session proposals. Also the most direct answer to the
geographic gap.

## Category 5: Fiscal and performance analytics

The **Ohio Auditor of State** audits 5,900+ state and local agencies, runs internal data
analytics work, and its **Local Government Services** arm consults on financial forecasting and
fiscal management for local governments. This is applied public-sector analysis at regional
scale, and it connects to the program-evaluation preference, which 2027 retains.

| Target | Contact |
|---|---|
| Ohio Auditor of State | ohioauditor.gov · Columbus regional office |

## Category 6: The data center and power corridor

Intel, Meta, Google, Microsoft, AWS and QTS across New Albany and Licking County represent
$50B+ in announced investment, plus AEP Ohio, whose grid capacity has become the central
regional data story. This is the infrastructure category in its most public form and the most
on-theme for a fifth-anniversary "what's next" program.

**Convert slowly.** Individual contacts at these companies are not publicly published, and we
are not sourcing them from brokers. Realistic routes are a warm introduction through a
Champion, the company's community or public affairs office, or meeting people at an industry
event. Do not expect this category to produce Champions before February.

**Sequencing note:** hold most of this until the CFP is live in October. "Come present at the
region's data conference" is a stronger opener to a stranger than "come volunteer for it."
Categories 1 and 2 are the exceptions, because the keynote window closes first and the ODEW
email costs nothing.

## A caution to carry forward

This list is built to counteract the fact that the existing Champion pool was recruited from
people already connected to Data Day, which reproduces the existing network and cuts against
the Regional Data Agenda's representation pillar. That only holds if these targets are actually
worked. If the list is not used, recruitment stays inside the same Franklin County circle it
started in.
