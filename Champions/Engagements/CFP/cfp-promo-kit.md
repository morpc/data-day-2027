## How this works

Two things, both quick. Do one or both.

1. **Share the call.** Post or forward it through your channels: email, LinkedIn, your
   organization's newsletter, a team meeting.
2. **Nudge two or three people.** Think of a colleague or team doing work the region
   should hear about, and ask them to submit.

MORPC staff review every proposal and make every selection. Your part ends once you've
shared or nudged.

**Please do not** promise anyone a slot, offer to review or score proposals, or share what
others have submitted. If your own team is submitting, that's great; staff handle the
review the same way as any other proposal.

## Why present at Data Day

Copy this into an email or post as-is, or trim it to fit.

> **Present at Data Day 2027.** Data Day is Central Ohio's annual conference for the
> public-sector data community, and 2027 is its fifth year. Each year it draws 200+ people
> from 75+ organizations across local governments, nonprofits, educational institutions,
> and civic-minded businesses. A 45-minute session puts your project, method, tool, or
> lesson learned in front of the practitioners and decision-makers who can use it. Every
> data topic and every skill level is welcome, and this year the program especially
> encourages proposals on data governance, data management, data analysis, and data
> infrastructure. Proposals are due Monday, November 30, 2026.

**Short version (one line):**

> Doing data work the region should hear about? Data Day 2027 wants your session
> proposal by November 30: <https://tinyurl.com/dataday-cfp>

### Reasons that land with hesitant presenters

- **Your work doesn't have to be finished or flashy.** Lessons learned, a method that
  saved time, or a project that went sideways all make strong sessions.
- **The room is your peers.** Attendees are people doing the same kind of work across the
  region, so practical detail is welcome.
- **Any level works.** Sessions range from "no technical background needed" to advanced.
- **The form is short.** A title, up to three objectives, a 100-word description, a
  300-word abstract, speakers, skill level, up to two topic tags, and how you'll engage
  the room.

## Nudging a potential presenter

A personal ask from you is far more persuasive than
any post.

### Email

> **Subject:** You should present at Data Day
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm helping spread the word about Data Day 2027, MORPC's annual conference for Central
> Ohio's data community. It's February 18, and the Call for Proposals is open through
> Monday, November 30.
>
> Your work on [specific project] came to mind right away. I think a lot of people in the
> room would want to hear how you [what they did / what they learned].
>
> Sessions are 45 minutes with plenty of time for questions, and the form is short:
> <https://tinyurl.com/dataday-cfp>
>
> No pressure either way, but I'd love to see you on the program.
>
> [Your name]

### LinkedIn message or chat

> Hi [Name], the Data Day 2027 Call for Proposals is open through November 30. Your work
> on [thing] would make a great session, and the form only takes a few minutes:
> https://tinyurl.com/dataday-cfp

### In person or by phone

> Have you thought about presenting at Data Day? Proposals are due November 30, and what

### Reminder, the week before the deadline

back, whether they can co-present, whether there's a speaker fee. The answer is the same
for all of them.
