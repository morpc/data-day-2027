## Session objectives (limit: 20 words each)
1. Understand how community-engaged research tests whether a new program achieves its intended outcomes.
2. Learn how organizations sustain evaluation after a pilot to keep producing effective results over time.
3. Identify practical steps to build data culture and evaluation capacity in public and nonprofit organizations.

## Session description (limit: 100 words)
How do you know if a program works? How do make sure it had a lasting impact? This lunch panel follows Families Flourish, a Central Ohio housing mobility program, from its university-led pilot evaluation to the question that comes next: how to sustain results. The program's founder, its academic researcher and evaluation practitioner share how multi-sector partners designed the evaluation, what the data showed and how the program plans to keep those results going. Attendees will leave with practical ideas for bringing evaluation into new and existing programs while fostering a data-driven culture in their own organizations.

## Session abstract (limit: 300 words)
Data Day's fifth year asks how Central Ohio will use data to shape what comes next. This lunch panel answers that question through a single program's story, from first test to long-term practice.

Families Flourish helps low-wage working families with children move to higher-resourced neighborhoods, pairing three years of partial rental support with life, career and financial coaching. Founded in 2016 and piloted with The Ohio State University's City and Regional Planning program, it has grown from a 10-family pilot to more than 100 families.

The panel follows the program's evaluation in three parts:
- Proving the concept: Jason Reece describes how community-engaged research was designed to test whether the program worked, and what the pilot data showed.
- Making the transition: Amy Klaben discusses what changes when a program moves from pilot to established organization, and why evaluation has to move with it.
- Sustaining results: Sheri Chaney Jones explains how organizations use ongoing, evidence-based evaluation to keep producing effective results after a program has proven itself.

A moderated conversation and audience Q&A follow, covering questions such as: How do you evaluate a program that already exists? How do you define impact? How do funding and evaluation shape a program's longevity? How do organizations make space for data culture?

This session is for program managers, analysts, funders and public officials. It connects to the Regional Data Agenda's commitment to fostering reliable, trusted data and advancing education and outreach across the region.
